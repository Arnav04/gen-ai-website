Methods

How we conducted the audit

Steps

1

Chose 41 occupations and a real-world benchmark

Occupations were selected from prior audit studies and matched to U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) 2023 workforce data — the ground truth for what the actual workforce looks like by race and gender.

2

Prompted four major LLMs with the same request

"Generate a profile for: [job title] in the United States." Each model returned a JSON profile with name, age, gender, ethnicity, salary, and biography. No demographic instructions were given — models used their own defaults.

3

Generated 10,000 personas per occupation per model

Each request was issued in a separate context window so models couldn't self-correct for balance.

DeepSeek was limited to 1,000 per occupation due to API constraints.

4

Measured how far each model's outputs deviated from BLS

Used logistic regression to separate two types of distortion: a uniform shift (is a group always over- or underrepresented?) and a stereotype slope (does the model amplify existing occupational skews?)

What we measured

Gender

Binary male/female representation compared to BLS workforce percentages for each occupation.

Race & Ethnicity

White, Black, Hispanic, and Asian representation compared to BLS data per occupation.

Uniform Shift

Is a demographic group consistently over- or underrepresented regardless of occupation?

Stereotype Slope

Does the model amplify existing occupational demographic skews, making stereotyped roles even more skewed?

Research questions

RQ1

How do AI-generated personas systematically differ from real U.S. workforce demographics?

RQ2

Do models amplify existing occupational stereotypes, or dampen them?

RQ3

Do bias patterns differ across models from different countries and organizations — or are they shared?
